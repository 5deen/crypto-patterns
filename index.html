Pattern-based verification

Passwords you can see.

crypto-patterns maps every character of a phrase to a coloured geometric shape and lays them out on a grid. Type the phrase again and the same grid comes back — pixel for pixel. Type it wrong and the pattern breaks.

How it works

Three steps, and only the middle one is secret.

Step one

Every character becomes a shape

A character's position in the alphabet picks its shape; how far it has wrapped past the shape list picks its colour. That is the entire glyph mapping — a pure function with no state, no randomness, and no lookup table to keep in sync.

See the mapping

Anyone can look at this. Without the mapping it is a picture of shapes.

Step two

The grid is public by design

The generated pattern is meant to be shown. Print it, pin it to a wall, put it on a login screen. What it does not carry is the mapping rules, and the grid on its own does not hand them over.

Step three

Verification is recognition

Re-enter the phrase and the pattern is regenerated from scratch. Because generation is deterministic, a correct phrase reproduces the original grid exactly — and a single wrong character visibly changes a cell.

Try it yourself

Type a phrase. The grid regenerates on every keystroke, entirely in your browser — nothing is sent anywhere.

Letters map to shapes; anything else renders as an empty cell.

What this is, and what it isn't

Being upfront about the threat model is part of the design.

Deterministic, always

The same phrase and the same mapping rules produce a byte-identical grid. Any randomness would have to be seeded from the phrase itself — never from a clock or a random number generator.

Recognisable at a glance

People are far better at spotting that two pictures differ than at comparing two strings of characters. The grid puts verification in the medium humans are good at.

Not a cipher

This is a visual encoding scheme, not a vetted cryptographic primitive. It has not been through cryptanalysis and should not stand in for a real cipher where one is needed.
